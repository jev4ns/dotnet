ICE Is Expanding in Massachusetts — and Healey Is Fighting Back
The tension between Beacon Hill and the Trump administration over immigration enforcement is escalating fast. Governor Maura Healey sent a formal letter to acting ICE Director Todd Lyons and DHS demanding a full accounting of every person arrested in Massachusetts since January 2025 — names, legal basis for arrest, detention locations, hearing dates, all of it, within one week. The response from federal officials was about what you'd expect: U.S. Attorney Leah Foley and Lyons fired back on X, accusing Healey of hypocrisy for demanding transparency from federal agencies while not sharing information with them.
Meanwhile, activists near the Burlington ICE field office spotted dozens of brand-new vehicles — paperwork visible in the windows pointing to the facility's address — stored in a nearby parking garage. It's not proof of an imminent surge, but it's not nothing either. Communities are clearly bracing: in Roslindale, some 225 volunteers fanned out across neighborhoods this weekend distributing 10,000 flyers on how to handle an ICE encounter, produced by a coalition spanning immigrant advocacy groups, the Boston Teachers Union, and local churches.
The political fight is real, but the on-the-ground anxiety is realer. With new vehicles being staged, a governor demanding answers, and neighborhoods organizing block by block, this story is moving fast in multiple directions at once. Watch for whether Healey gets any response to her letter before her one-week deadline — and whether any legal confrontation between the state and federal government follows.

What Else You Should Know
A Somerville eyesore is finally becoming housing — a lot of it.
The long-abandoned Star Market on Broadway in Winter Hill, which has been an empty shell since the mid-2000s, has been demolished and a four-developer team has broken ground on 319 apartments across two six-story buildings, plus retail and a new public plaza. A significant portion will be affordable units. Mayor Jake Wilson, a Winter Hill native, is reportedly thrilled. This is exactly the kind of infill development the state's housing crisis demands, and it's nice when it actually happens.

Marshfield is playing chicken with the SJC — and its own grant money.
The Marshfield Select Board voted 2-1 to put the MBTA Communities zoning bylaw back on the April Town Meeting warrant, just 48 hours before Town Counsel was set to argue the town's case against the state mandate before the Supreme Judicial Court. Vice Chair Steve Darcy warned that pulling the compliant zoning before a court ruling could cost the town millions in state grants. This is one of the higher-stakes local showdowns over the MBTA zoning law, and the timing is genuinely wild.

Two Boston councilors want to scrap the Blue Hill Avenue bus lane project.
City Councilors Miniard Culpepper and Brian Worrell are calling on the city and the MBTA to cancel the roughly $44 million planned center bus lanes on Blue Hill Avenue and redirect the money to keep fare-free service running on the 23, 28, and 29 bus routes. A third councilor, Enrique Pepén, blocked a full council vote on the resolution — saying he supports free fares but wants more conversation first. This is a real tension between two genuinely good transit goals, and it won't be resolved quickly.

Boston police are warning about a parking ticket scam that looks very official.
Fraudulent parking violation notices — complete with a judge's name, a court address, a hearing date, and a Massachusetts state seal — are circulating in the city. The tell: a QR code prompting payment. Don't scan it. Verify any ticket at the city's official website, and report anything suspicious to CrimeStoppers at 1-800-494-TIPS.

Chelmsford officers will be wearing body cameras by July 1.
A $122,769 state grant will put body cams on all 50-plus officers in the Chelmsford Police Department, with the backing of officers, supervisors, and all three police unions. The funding comes from the state's Law Enforcement Body-Worn Camera Program, which has distributed nearly $13 million statewide since 2022. Broad union support for body cameras is notable — it doesn't always go this smoothly.

One More Thing

An Easton police officer and two bystanders pulled a 78-year-old man out of a burning pickup truck at 2 in the morning on Route 138 — and body camera footage captured the whole thing. Officer Dean Soucie broke the driver's side window while a passing stranger climbed into the cab to help free the man. The driver, Francis Leverone of Raynham, survived with a dislocated hip. On a heavy news Sunday, this one's worth a minute of your time.
