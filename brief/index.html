The Lead
A Massachusetts Sailor Got Hurt. Here's What That Tells You About Where We Are.

Over the weekend, the United States and Israel launched military strikes against Iran — and Massachusetts felt it personally. A young man who graduated from Lunenburg High School in 2022 and works as a police dispatcher in Sudbury was injured in the fighting while serving with the U.S. Navy. His department says he's going to be okay, but the moment is a gut-punch reminder that the biggest story in the world right now has names and faces attached to it — including some from right here.

The conflict is already rippling into everyday life in ways that will hit your wallet soon. Oil tankers are avoiding the Strait of Hormuz, through which about 20 percent of the world's oil flows. International crude jumped nearly 7 percent Monday. Gas in Massachusetts is hovering around $2.92 a gallon right now, but that's expected to climb. And New England has an added exposure: Qatar has halted liquefied natural gas production because of the war, and this region depends heavily on LNG shipments — especially in winter. The timing could not be worse.

On Beacon Hill, Republican U.S. Senate candidate John Deaton — a Bolton Marine veteran running against Senator Ed Markey — is calling for clearer communication between the Trump administration and Congress about the strikes, saying he needs more information before he can assess whether they were the right call. That's a careful position for a candidate trying to thread the needle in a blue state. Expect this conflict to become a defining issue in Massachusetts's federal races this cycle.

What Else You Should Know

State employees are losing GLP-1 weight-loss drug coverage. In a narrow 10-7 vote, the Massachusetts Group Insurance Commission — which covers more than 460,000 state employees — voted to end coverage of GLP-1 drugs like Ozempic for weight loss. The $46 million annual price tag, combined with Gov. Maura Healey's push to find $100 million in savings, drove the decision. Critics warn it could widen health disparities and cost more down the road if untreated conditions worsen.

Healey and Campbell tell schools: ICE needs a real warrant. After federal agents allegedly lied about searching for a missing child to gain access to a Columbia University building and arrest a student, Governor Healey and Attorney General Andrea Campbell issued joint guidance reminding Massachusetts schools and colleges that ICE cannot enter non-public areas — dorms, labs, staff offices — without a valid judicial warrant. They're also urging schools to post clear signage marking those protected spaces.

Boston.com →

Federal housing cuts could put nearly 4,000 Massachusetts households at risk. The Trump administration wants to gut "Housing First" programs — the two-decade-old model that prioritizes getting people housed before addressing addiction or mental illness. That could mean $91 million in federal funding disappears, forcing some Massachusetts programs to shrink or close. In Northampton, a medically-intensive supportive housing program serving 14 of the state's most vulnerable residents is already declining to take new clients. This is not an abstract policy debate for those 4,000 households.

Ayanna Pressley endorses Ed Markey. Representative Pressley — who herself seriously considered challenging Senator Markey before deciding not to run — is now backing his reelection bid against fellow Democratic Representative Seth Moulton. Pressley cited their joint work on immigration and police accountability. It's a meaningful signal to progressive voters, though Moulton is still making the generational-change argument that got Pressley herself to Congress back in 2018.

Boston Globe →

The SJC is weighing whether 18-to-20-year-olds can carry handguns in Massachusetts. The state's highest court heard arguments Monday in a case stemming from the 2021 arrest of Mikai Thomson, who was 20 when police found a handgun in his car. Gun-rights advocates argue Massachusetts's law limiting carry licenses to those 21 and older violates the Second Amendment under the Supreme Court's 2022 Bruen decision. A ruling against the state would be a significant shift in Massachusetts firearms law.

Boston.com →

Healey's universal pre-K promise is running into reality. The governor pledged that by end of 2026, every family of a 4-year-old in one of Massachusetts's 26 Gateway Cities would have access to free or low-cost preschool. But seven of those cities haven't even joined the state grant program yet, and providers in participating cities say the challenges of actually implementing universal pre-K on the ground are substantial. The legislature has put in about $60 million; Healey's FY2027 budget asks for $32 million more. Whether that's enough — and fast enough — is a real question.
