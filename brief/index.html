The Lead
Healey Wants $4.9 Billion Back — And She Knows She Won't Get It

Governor Maura Healey sent a letter to U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent this week demanding the federal government refund Massachusetts households $1,745 each — a total of roughly $4.9 billion — to cover what residents paid in extra costs while President Trump's broad tariffs were in effect. The Supreme Court struck down those tariffs last month in a 6-3 ruling, finding that Trump had overstepped his emergency powers authority. Healey's math is drawn from a congressional estimate of tariff-driven consumer costs between February 2025 and January 2026.

Here's the thing: Healey knows this isn't happening. Speaking Wednesday before the Greater Boston Chamber of Commerce, she said plainly, "I don't expect to be paid, but I am trying to make a point." And it's an effective one. Massachusetts is already the second most expensive state in the country — a fact her own economic development secretary was lamenting at a budget hearing this week. Every dollar squeezed out of residents by policies the courts ultimately called unconstitutional is real money that didn't go toward rent, groceries, or tuition.

What happens next is the more consequential question. Trump has already responded to the Supreme Court ruling by announcing new 15% global tariffs under a different legal authority. Massachusetts is among 24 states that have sued to block those. The tariff fight is far from over — and Healey is clearly positioning herself as one of its loudest opponents.

🔬 Gillette Staying Put — With a $1 Billion Glow-Up

Procter & Gamble announced Tuesday that Gillette's corporate headquarters isn't leaving South Boston. Instead, the company plans to spend $1 billion building a brand-new Grooming Headquarters and Technical Innovation Center at 232 A St. in Fort Point, right next door to its current campus. The company has been on that waterfront site for over a century, and while manufacturing is shifting to Andover, the brainpower stays here.

⚛️ Nuclear Power: From Fringe to Serious Conversation in Massachusetts

Governor Healey's push to add nuclear energy to the state's clean energy mix is gaining unexpected traction. A working group leading a nuclear roadmap initiative — prompted partly by federal hostility toward offshore wind — held more than 1,000 stakeholder meetings and found the public far more open to nuclear than expected. The catch: new nuclear plants are notoriously slow and expensive to build, and electricity prices in Massachusetts are already eye-watering.

🏫 Charter Schools Must Follow Public Records Law, SJC Rules

The state's Supreme Judicial Court issued a clear ruling Tuesday: Massachusetts charter schools are public schools, which means they must comply with the state's public records law. The case arose after Malden's Mystic Valley Regional Charter School refused to hand over documents requested by members of the public. The Attorney General's office had backed the records request. Transparency advocates are calling it a significant win.

💊 Healey on Arrested Aide: 'I Wish He'd Never Been Hired'

Governor Healey didn't mince words Tuesday about LaMar Cook, a former state aide who pleaded not guilty last week to drug trafficking and illegal firearm possession charges. Prosecutors say a digital scale with traces of fentanyl, heroin, cocaine, and cutting agents was found in a cabinet at his Springfield state office. Cook, who was fired last October, has since posted bail. His attorney says the bail amount set was appropriate; prosecutors had sought four times as much.

🏙️ Massachusetts Is Bleeding Young People — And Knows It

At a Joint Ways and Means budget hearing this week, Healey's Secretary of Economic Development Eric Paley offered a frank assessment: Massachusetts is losing residents to other states, and the outmigration is sharpest among 22-to-30-year-olds. "These are the people we really want here," he said. The state ranks second most expensive in the nation, and officials acknowledge there's only so much the budget can do about that.

🏙️ Wu Announces $4.4M Partnership for Immigrant Services

Mayor Michelle Wu unveiled a new public-private partnership Tuesday combining $3.1 million from the Boston Foundation, Barr Foundation, and United Way with $1.3 million in city grants to support immigrant families. The funding covers legal services, mental health support, "know your rights" education, and basic necessities. Wu framed it as a direct response to the Trump administration's immigration enforcement posture: "We will not let anyone storm into Boston and disrupt our community."
