Healey to Aviation Company: Stop Fueling ICE's Deportation Flights

Governor Maura Healey sent a blunt letter Friday to Tony Lefebvre, CEO of Signature Aviation — a Florida-based company that serves as the sole fixed-base operator at Hanscom Field in Bedford — demanding that the company immediately cut ties with ICE and stop enabling deportation flights out of Massachusetts. Healey didn't mince words: "Enough is enough." She laid out exactly what Signature does to make those flights happen — fueling the planes, opening the gates, escorting ICE vehicles to the tarmac — and told Lefebvre flatly that without Signature, ICE couldn't operate out of Hanscom at all. According to Healey's office, 132 ICE flights departed from Hanscom during the first year of Trump's second term, including 13 in January alone.

This is a pressure campaign, not a legal order — Healey has no authority to compel a private company to drop a federal contract. But that's partly the point. By naming Signature publicly and making the moral case directly to its CEO, Healey is trying to embarrass the company into action and signal to Massachusetts residents that she's fighting the Trump administration's immigration enforcement on every front she can find. Whether Signature responds is an open question; the company hadn't replied to press inquiries as of Monday. What happens next: watch for Signature's response (or silence), and expect this letter to become campaign fodder as Healey heads into her reelection race.

🔥 Heating Oil Hits $4.70 a Gallon — and Community Action Agencies Are Sounding the Alarm

The war in the Middle East has sent home heating oil prices surging — up nearly 30 percent in a week, reaching as high as $4.70 per gallon in parts of the state. Gas prices jumped 43 cents in just seven days, according to AAA. Community Action agencies, which serve more than 160,000 households across Massachusetts, are urgently asking Governor Healey and the Legislature for a $35 million emergency fuel assistance supplemental — warning that tens of thousands of vulnerable residents have already exhausted their winter fuel benefits with no relief in sight. This is a genuine crisis unfolding right now, in real homes, with real consequences.

📉 Healey's Cabinet Is Hemorrhaging — And Her Opponents Are Noticing

Seven of Governor Healey's twelve Cabinet secretaries have left in the past year alone — an exodus that outpaces her two predecessors at the same point in their first terms. The Education and Housing secretary slots were so recently vacant that their pages had disappeared from the administration's own website. Healey's team attributes the churn to the brutal demands of governing in the Trump era; her Republican opponents call it a sign of dysfunction at the top. Either way, it's a storyline that will follow her into the reelection campaign.

🗳️ Galvin Agrees to Release Jail-Based Voting Data — After a Lawsuit Made Him

Secretary of State William Galvin settled a lawsuit Monday, agreeing to release long-overdue reports by June 1 showing how many incarcerated people in Massachusetts requested and received ballots during the 2024 elections. Lawyers for Civil Rights and the Campaign Legal Center had sued after Galvin's office failed to produce data required by the 2022 VOTES Act — for both the September primary and the November presidential election. With thousands of incarcerated Massachusetts residents potentially eligible to vote in state elections, advocates say the data is essential to knowing whether the law is actually working.

⚡ House Speaker Mariano Admits the Energy Bill Won't Help Much This Winter

House Speaker Ron Mariano had a refreshingly candid moment on WCVB's On the Record Sunday: when asked how the House's newly passed energy bill would help residents next winter, he said, "The completely honest answer is it's not going to help much at all." The bill, which passed 128-27, aims to strip away some of the "public policy charges" that have piled onto electric and gas bills over two decades of clean energy incentives — fees that now account for roughly 20 percent of a typical customer's bill. Long-term savings are the goal; short-term relief is not on the menu.

⚖️ Matthew Farwell Trial to Proceed — Judge Rejects Dismissal Bid

A federal judge ruled Monday that the murder case against Matthew Farwell, a former Stoughton police detective accused of killing Sandra Birchmore in 2021, is legally sound and will move forward. US District Court Chief Judge Denise Casper rejected Farwell's motion to dismiss, finding the indictment properly states the elements of the crime. Farwell, who allegedly began sexually exploiting Birchmore when she was 15, is accused of strangling her and staging her death as a suicide. Trial is set for October.

One More Thing

Speaker Mariano's candor about the energy bill not helping much this winter is the kind of thing politicians usually spend three paragraphs trying to avoid saying. Credit where it's due — even if the honest answer is a cold one. With heating oil hitting $4.70 a gallon and a war driving prices higher by the day, Massachusetts residents could use a lot more of that straight talk, and a lot faster.
