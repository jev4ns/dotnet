Governor Healey Demands ICE Answers as Detainee Dies in Arizona Prison

The human cost of the federal immigration crackdown landed hard in Massachusetts this week. Emmanuel Damas, a 56-year-old Boston man detained by ICE, died in a for-profit detention facility in Arizona after complaints about a toothache went ignored — an infected tooth that spread until it killed him. At nearly the same moment, Governor Maura Healey fired off a letter to outgoing DHS Secretary Kristi Noem and Acting ICE Director Todd Lyons demanding a full accounting of every ICE arrest in the state since January 2025. Healey's numbers are striking: of the 1,461 people arrested during last May's "Operation Patriot" surge, nearly half — 671 people — had no criminal record. In the September surge, that figure climbed to more than 60 percent.

Healey's letter is part pressure campaign, part paper trail. With the PROTECT Act moving through the Legislature — a bill that would expand courthouse protections and due process rights for immigrants — the governor is clearly trying to force transparency from an administration that has provided it on almost nothing. The Statehouse hearing on the PROTECT Act this week drew an overflow crowd, including Brenda Romero of Chelsea, whose husband was arrested by federal agents as he walked out of a courthouse moments after being granted asylum.

What happens next depends partly on the courts, partly on the Legislature, and partly on whether the Trump administration bothers to respond to Healey at all. Given its track record, don't hold your breath on that last one.

AG Campbell Wins Court Order Forcing FEMA to Restore Disaster Funds

Attorney General Andrea Campbell and a coalition of 23 states secured a federal court order Friday requiring FEMA to reinstate the Building Resilient Infrastructure and Communities (BRIC) program after the Trump administration ignored a previous court order from December. The program funds flood, wildfire, and power outage mitigation projects across the country — the kind of infrastructure work that saves money and lives long before disaster strikes. Campbell was blunt: the administration should have complied months ago.

Gateway Cities Tax Credits Are Going to Cities That Don't Qualify

Here's a quiet budget story with real consequences: Massachusetts has handed out more than $10 million in housing development tax credits to Salem and Quincy under the Gateway Cities program — even though both cities stopped meeting the program's educational attainment criteria years ago (Quincy in 2016, Salem in 2020). Meanwhile, Weymouth and Marlborough, which do meet all three qualifying criteria, have been locked out of the program since Beacon Hill last updated the list back in 2013. Someone needs to fix this.

Marshfield Takes Its MBTA Zoning Fight to the SJC

Marshfield town attorney Robert Galvin stood before the state Supreme Judicial Court this week arguing that the MBTA Communities Act is an unfunded mandate that coerces towns into approving zoning changes under threat of losing state grants. The state's assistant AG pushed back, saying administrative costs are all the law actually requires. The justices have 130 days to rule — and whatever they decide will reverberate through every South Shore town still wrestling with multi-family zoning requirements. In the meantime, Marshfield's Select Board voted to put the issue back before April town meeting.

Former Healey Aide Arraigned on Cocaine Charges

LaMar Cook, who served as deputy director of Governor Healey's Western Massachusetts Office, was arraigned Friday in Hampden Superior Court on charges that he received eight kilograms of cocaine at a state office building in Springfield while still on the job. Cook was arrested last October and his case was bumped up to Superior Court last month. The next hearing is April 3. The Healey administration has said little publicly about the case.

Two Boston Restaurants to Pay $422K Over Mislabeled Service Fees

Carrie Nation Cocktail Club and The Dubliner, both under the East Coast Tavern Group, will pay more than $422,000 in restitution and penalties after the AG's office found they charged customers a "service fee" for over a year but kept the money instead of distributing it to the 84 servers and bartenders who were owed it. The restaurants say a point-of-sale system mislabeled what was meant to be a general "hospitality fee." The distinction matters legally — and financially — to the workers who didn't see a dime of it.

A Scituate Man Is Headed to Superior Court for Murder

A Plymouth County grand jury indicted Christopher Caron, 42, on Friday for the murder of Declan Perry, 27, of Portland, Maine. Prosecutors say Caron and Perry drove to Boston to buy fentanyl and cocaine, returned to Scituate, and when Perry died in the early morning hours, Caron wrapped his body in moving blankets and duct-taped it before dropping it off in a car at South Shore Hospital in Weymouth — staging it to look like an overdose. The case now moves to Superior Court.
