Energy Regulation

Pipeline Safety in Massachusetts

The DPU's Pipeline Safety Division ensures that natural gas distribution companies, municipal gas departments, steam distribution companies and other intrastate operators comply with state and federal pipeline safety regulations, and enforces the state's Dig Safe law to prevent excavation damage.

01

Gas pipelines

Inspection and enforcement for the companies and municipal departments that operate the state's gas distribution systems.

  • Standard, construction and integrity management inspections
  • Operator qualification and incident investigations
  • Dig Safe enforcement and damage-prevention data
Source: PHMSA 2023 evaluation ↗

02

LNG facilities

Inspection of liquefied natural gas facilities under federal Part 193 standards, including procedures, records and field transfer operations.

  • Part 193 LNG inspections on a scheduled cycle
  • 2024 LNG facility inspection observed by PHMSA as part of the state's program evaluation
Source: PHMSA 2023 evaluation ↗

03

Networked geothermal

In July 2024 the Division issued Massachusetts' first safety guidelines for utility networked geothermal systems, closed-loop networks that connect ground-source heat pumps.

  • Notice of intent before construction
  • Emergency response and operator qualification plans
  • Annual reporting and periodic inspections
Source: DPU press release ↗

Background

About Me

I'm a Whitman native with a Master's in Energy Systems and a BS in Mechanical Engineering from Northeastern University. My career has focused on the intersection of energy infrastructure, public safety and policy, first as an engineer and now as a regulator.

I'm an Assistant Director of the Pipeline Safety Division at the Massachusetts Department of Public Utilities, where I've worked for seven years. The Division earned a perfect score on PHMSA's 2023 evaluation of the state program and issued Massachusetts' first safety guidelines for utility networked geothermal systems.

In 2024, I founded South Shore News to solve a problem I saw in local government: important decisions being made without public awareness. It uses AI tools to process meeting video and draft coverage of 19 communities that traditional media has largely left behind.

I've served on the Whitman Select Board since 2019 and currently chair it.

I live in Whitman with my wife and daughter. When I'm not at work or in a town meeting, you'll find me cooking, working on home electrification projects, or walking my dog.

Views expressed on this site are my own and do not represent the Massachusetts Department of Public Utilities.
